# Learning Process

## 1. How to Learn Faster with the Feynman Technique

**Question 1: What is the Feynman Technique? (1 line)**

The Feynman Technique is learning a concept by explaining it in simple words, as if teaching a beginner, and then going back to the source to fill any gaps.

## 3. Learning How to Learn (Barbara Oakley TED Talk)

**Question 2: What was the most interesting story or idea for you?**

Barbara Oakley's own story of struggling with math and science early on, and later learning to study in a new way that made her an engineering professor, showed me that being bad at a subject is not a fixed trait.

**Question 3: What are active and diffused modes of thinking?**

- **Focused (active) mode:** concentrated attention on one problem, working through it step by step.
- **Diffuse mode:** relaxed, wide-ranging thinking that makes connections, often while resting, walking, or doing something else.

Both modes matter. Switching between them helps with learning and with solving hard problems.

## 4. Learn Anything in 20 Hours

**Question 4: What are the steps to take when approaching a new topic? (points only)**

1. Break the skill down into smaller parts.
2. Learn enough to be able to self-correct.
3. Remove barriers that stop you from practicing.
4. Commit to practicing for at least 20 hours.

## Learning Process Going Forward

**Question 5: What actions can you take to improve your learning process?**

- Work in focused blocks (for example, 25 minutes) with short breaks, and keep my phone out of reach during them.
- After each topic, explain it in my own words, as if to a beginner, and note where I got stuck.
- Write notes in my own words instead of copying.
- Build a small project for each concept so I practice it, not just read about it.
- Track how much time I actually spend on each topic.
- Review older topics regularly so they don't fade.
- Ask for help early when I'm blocked instead of spending hours stuck.
